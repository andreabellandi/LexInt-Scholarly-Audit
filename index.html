Can a dictionary explain why it changed — and why it did not?

A guided reading of a recorded LexInt experiment. No RDF knowledge, database installation, or programming is required.

A scholarly dictionary should preserve more than its final answer. It should show the evidence considered, the editorial decision, what was actually published, and what remains open to debate.

How to read this page. This demonstrator explains the corrected experimental run of 21 November 2026 (dsh2027-corrected-20261121-77d2c3e). It does not execute tests or connect to GraphDB. Outcomes are grounded in the delivered evaluation records and independent checks of the RDF graph differences and SPARQL answers. The interpretations and editorial choices about razza are hypothetical: the experiment tests procedural correctness, not historical truth. The claims concern a controlled test setting rather than a real editorial history.

Tests T02-T04 · suspended scholarly interpretation

Documenting a question without imposing an answer

The word razza appears in radically different ideological contexts in a Fascist document from 1938 and Article 3 of the Italian Constitution, in force from 1948. But this contrast alone does not establish two lexical senses.

Historical testimony · 1938

Fascist racial discourse

A consultation record points to the relevant historical material. Its political claims are documentary evidence, not endorsed lexical knowledge.

Historical testimony · 1948

Why this matters: checking the completeness of scholarly records is different from checking that editorial decisions were faithfully executed. The experiment does not claim that arbitrary publication-integrity rules are impossible to express using advanced SHACL approaches.

What this demonstrator does and does not establish

Recorded run: 12/12 tests passed; 7/7 competency-question answer sets exactly matched their frozen oracle; 3/3 intentionally corrupted counterexamples were detected. T09 included a failure after provisional writes on GraphDB 10.8.14; following rollback, five checked named graphs were unchanged. The code was tied to commit 77d2c3e with a reported clean worktree. Outside this test's scope: strong institutional identity, multi-writer concurrency, human usability studies, and the philological truth of the etymological or semantic claims.
